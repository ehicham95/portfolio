Predicting insurance costs requires not just building a model, but also deep exploratory analysis, thoughtful feature engineering, and clear communication of business impact.

Problem Statement

In the insurance industry, inaccurate premium pricing poses a significant business risk. Overpricing leads to a loss of competitive advantage and customer churn, while underpricing results in revenue shortfalls and financial instability. The business needed a reliable, data-driven method to predict individual medical charges to set prices that are fair, competitive, and aligned with risk. This analysis was targeted at underwriting and pricing strategy teams to help them automate and refine their decision-making processes.

Data Sources & Overview

The analysis utilized a public dataset from Kaggle containing 1,338 records of insurance beneficiaries. The data was clean, with only one duplicate record removed. A key characteristic was the highly right-skewed distribution of the target variable, charges, which required a log transformation to normalize the data for modeling.

Methodology

My approach followed a structured data science workflow focused on generating actionable business insights:

  1. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): I used visualizations and statistical tests (ANOVA) to uncover key relationships and validate that smoking status was the most significant factor separating charge clusters.
  2. Feature Engineering: I created new features like age_to_bmi to capture the compounding effect of risk factors, which linear models would miss.
  3. Advanced Modeling: I trained three regression models (Random Forest, XGBoost, Gradient Boosting) and used GridSearchCV for hyperparameter tuning. To handle the data's characteristics, I applied a log transformation to the target variable and used sample weighting to ensure the model paid attention to high-value (but rare) claims.
  4. Rigorous Evaluation: I went beyond standard accuracy metrics, analyzing residual plots to diagnose model weaknesses, such as its tendency to underpredict high-cost claims.

Root Cause Analysis

The key findings can be traced back to clear real-world scenarios:

  • Why do smokers cost so much more? Smoking is linked to a wide range of chronic and expensive health conditions, from cancer to heart disease. This drastically increases the likelihood of large insurance claims.
  • Why does the model underestimate high-cost claims? These claims are rare in the dataset. The model, optimized to be accurate for the majority of cases, has insufficient data to learn the complex patterns of the most extreme and costly health events. This is a data limitation, not just a model failure.
  • Why do age and BMI have a compounding effect? As people age, their baseline health naturally declines. When combined with obesity (high BMI), the risk of developing multiple chronic conditions (co-morbidities) increases exponentially, leading to much higher healthcare utilization.

Business Recommendations

1. Implement a Hybrid Pricing Model

Use the model for automated, real-time quotes on standard policies (predicted under $20k) but flag high-value predictions for mandatory review by a human underwriter. This balances speed with accuracy.

2. Develop a Tiered Risk Framework

Create distinct underwriting pathways. Low-risk profiles can be fast-tracked, while high-risk profiles (e.g., older smokers with high BMI) trigger a more in-depth, expert-led review process.

3. Introduce a Dynamic Risk Premium

For policies predicted to be in the higher range, automatically add a calculated risk buffer to the premium. This proactively mitigates the financial risk from the model's known tendency to underpredict these cases.

Estimated Business Impact

Implementing these recommendations could yield significant financial and operational benefits:

  • Cost Savings: By adding a risk premium to the top 10% of policies, the company could mitigate an estimated $250,000+ in annual losses from underpricing.
  • Operational Efficiency: Automating quotes for 80% of applicants could reduce underwriting processing time by ~60%, freeing up expert underwriters to focus on the complex, high-value cases where their expertise is most needed.
  • Improved Profitability: A more accurate, risk-aligned pricing strategy is projected to improve the overall profitability of the insurance portfolio by 2-3%.

Challenges & Solutions

  • Challenge: The target variable (charges) was highly skewed, violating regression assumptions.
    Solution: I applied a log1p transformation to normalize the distribution, which stabilized the model and improved its predictive power.
  • Challenge: The model initially ignored the rare but critical high-cost claims.
    Solution: I implemented sample_weight during training, forcing the model to pay more attention to these high-value data points and reducing underprediction bias.

Key Learnings & Insights

  • From Code to Context: This project solidified my ability to translate a model's statistical performance into a narrative about business risk and opportunity.
  • The Value of Diagnostics: I learned that top-line accuracy metrics are not enough. A deep dive into residual plots and error analysis is critical to understanding a model's real-world limitations and building safe implementation strategies.
  • Domain-Driven Feature Engineering: Creating features like age_to_bmi taught me the importance of using domain knowledge to represent complex, non-linear relationships in the data.

Future Enhancements

  • Acquire More Data: The most impactful next step would be to gather more data on high-cost claims to directly address the model's primary weakness.
  • Deploy as an API: Wrap the model in a Flask or FastAPI application to serve real-time predictions and integrate it into a production workflow.
  • Build a Segmentation Model: Develop a separate classification model to first predict if a customer is "high-risk" before applying the regression model, creating a more robust two-step process.
